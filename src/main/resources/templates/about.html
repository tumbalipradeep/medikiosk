About MediKiosk

A serious intake platform for the SIH26047 problem

MediKiosk addresses the overcrowding of outpatient care by moving the routine case-taking step to a kiosk: patients record their concern in their own words, the platform structures it into a clinical case, and a physician reviews it with full traceability.

What MediKiosk does

The canonical journey is Identify  →  Converse  →  Scan  →  Summarize & Route  →  Consult. A patient verifies their local kiosk identity, holds a guided conversation in a supported language, attaches reports, and reviews everything before submission. A physician then opens a structured clinical workspace — the patient's own answers, extracted documents with provenance, system flags that always require clinical review, and a place to record the assessment.

MediKiosk assists in information collection, structuring, extraction, and summarization. It is not an autonomous diagnostician and never presents itself as one.

Engineering principles

  • ✓ Honest capabilities. OCR, handwriting recognition, and external transmission report their true status; nothing is silently presented as working.
  • ✓ Conservative clinical logic. Missing data is never labelled normal; red flags are markers that require a clinician, not diagnoses.
  • ✓ Security by enforcement. Role-based access is enforced in the backend; frontend hiding is never treated as authorization.
  • ✓ Auditability. Significant actions leave a persisted audit event an administrator can inspect.
  • ✓ Accessible by design. Keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, and text scaling are part of the foundation.

A public-facing website

Normal web pages for the product, its features, privacy posture, and contact — not a dashboard.

Role-appropriate experiences

Patients get clarity; physicians get a clinical workspace; administrators get platform control with an honest view of capability status.

A local demo deployment

This deployment uses a local, non-ABHA identity and transmits nothing externally. It is intended for evaluation, not live clinical use.
